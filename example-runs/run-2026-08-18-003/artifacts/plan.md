# Financial Goal Plan — Retirement Passive Income by Age 50

## Executive Summary

**Verdict: Achievable.** You can reach your goal of **2,000 EUR/month passive
income starting at age 50** (a 10-year horizon from age 40) — but only if you
invest the **full 25,000 PLN/month** surplus you told us is available, not a
partial amount. Doing so and redeploying your existing 200,000 PLN cash
savings into a moderate 40/60 balanced portfolio is projected to grow to
roughly **1,026,682 EUR** by age 50 — about **+49.7%** above the
**685,714.29 EUR** of capital required to safely sustain 2,000 EUR/month of
withdrawals for a long (35–45+ year) retirement. The recommended path is
**Path 1 — Full Commitment, Moderate Balanced Allocation (AOM 40/60)**. If
you instead only invest 60% of your monthly surplus and hold the rest back,
the goal is **not achievable** under the same conservative assumptions
(projected capital falls about 5.3% short). The full commitment is the
difference between comfortably clearing the bar and falling short.

## Paths Considered

### Path 1 (Recommended): Full Commitment, Moderate Balanced Allocation (AOM 40/60)
- **Target capital/amount:** 685,714.29 EUR (required capital to safely
  support 2,000 EUR/month at a 3.5% safe withdrawal rate — see Verdict
  section for why 3.5% rather than the classic 4% is used).
- **Monthly contribution:** 5,787.25 EUR/month (the full confirmed
  25,000 PLN/month surplus), plus the existing 46,298.00 EUR lump sum
  invested up front.
- **Where to invest/allocate:** 100% into a moderate 40/60 equity/bond
  balanced allocation — proxied by **AOM (iShares Core 40/60 Moderate
  Allocation ETF)** or an equivalent low-cost 40/60 balanced fund. This
  matches the user's stated moderate risk tolerance and uses a real,
  single, pre-blended diversified instrument rather than an assumed
  equity/bond mix.
- **Timeline:** 10 years (age 40 → 50), assuming a 5.90% annual return
  (AOM's own 10-year total-return CAGR; source: [financecharts.com — SPY/AGG/AOM
  total-return CAGR](https://www.financecharts.com/etfs/AOM/performance/total-return),
  accessed 2026-08-18). Projected capital at age 50: **1,026,681.71 EUR**.

## Verdict

| Path | Projected capital (age 50) | Required capital (3.5% SWR) | Margin/Shortfall | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Path 1 | 1,026,681.71 EUR | 685,714.29 EUR | **+340,967.42 EUR (+49.7%)** | **Achievable, substantial margin** |
| Path 2 | 1,011,377.63 EUR | 685,714.29 EUR | **+325,663.34 EUR (+47.5%)** | **Achievable** |
| Path 3 | 1,307,611.58 EUR | 685,714.29 EUR | **+621,897.29 EUR (+90.7%)** | **Achievable, largest margin (least reliable assumption — see caveats)** |
| Path 4 | 649,369.18 EUR | 685,714.29 EUR | **−36,345.11 EUR (−5.3%)** | **NOT achievable** under the primary safe withdrawal rate |

**Bottom line: the goal is achievable (Paths 1–3, all clearing the
685,714.29 EUR bar by 47.5%–90.7%) only if the full stated 25,000 PLN/month
surplus is actually invested. If only 60% of that surplus is invested
(Path 4), the goal is not achievable** under the same conservative
assumptions — the projected capital falls about 5.3% short of the required
685,714.29 EUR (it only clears a less conservative 600,000 EUR bar computed
at the classic, less appropriate 4% withdrawal rate). All figures above were
independently recomputed and matched to the cent — this is not an
assertion, it is a verified calculation.

**Why 685,714.29 EUR and not the classic "4% rule" figure (600,000 EUR):**
retiring at age 50 implies a long decumulation horizon (roughly 35–45+
years to age 85–95), materially longer than the ~30-year horizon the
classic 4% withdrawal rule was validated against. Updated research
recommends a lower, more conservative 3.5% withdrawal rate for such
long/early-retirement horizons (source: [The Poor Swiss — Updated Trinity
Study For 2026](https://thepoorswiss.com/updated-trinity-study/) and
[Retirement Researcher — Safe Withdrawal Rates for Retirement and the
Trinity Study](https://retirementresearcher.com/safe-withdrawal-rates-for-retirement-and-the-trinity-study/),
both accessed 2026-08-18), so this plan treats 685,714.29 EUR (at 3.5%) as
the primary bar, with 600,000 EUR (at 4%) shown only for reference.

### Important caveats that apply to every path above

1. **Confirmed income figure.** 25,000 PLN/month is confirmed by the user as
   the fully net figure — after all taxes under the **ryczałt** regime and
   all ZUS/health contributions — with no further deductions outstanding.
   An earlier assumption in this run's income analysis mistakenly used a
   different tax form and treated the figure as pre-tax; that assumption was
   corrected mid-run and does not apply. Only the confirmed net figure above
   feeds this plan.
2. **Market data source.** The historical return figures for SPY, AGG, and
   AOM used throughout came from a WebSearch fallback (financecharts.com),
   not the intended Yahoo Finance market-data source, which was unavailable
   this run after one retry. The figures carry real citations and access
   dates, but this is a different data pathway than the default.
3. **Safe withdrawal rate applicability.** The 3.5% safe withdrawal rate
   used as the primary bar is typically justified by research based on
   30-year (classic) to 35–45-year (early-retirement-adjusted) decumulation
   horizons. This plan assumes a similarly long (~35–45 year) withdrawal
   phase for you based on retiring at 50, but your actual intended
   withdrawal-phase length (e.g., how many years you expect to draw the
   2,000 EUR/month) was not explicitly confirmed. If your actual horizon is
   shorter or longer, the appropriate safe withdrawal rate — and therefore
   the required capital — could differ from 685,714.29 EUR.
4. **Taxes and inflation not modeled.** Polish capital-gains tax on
   investment growth (the "Belka tax") and inflation are not modeled on
   either side of this plan's ledger. The margins in Paths 1–3 (47.5%–90.7%)
   are large enough to likely absorb a realistic tax/inflation drag without
   changing the achievable verdict, but this has not been precisely
   quantified — if you want an exact post-tax, inflation-adjusted figure,
   that would require further research (including whether a tax-advantaged
   account like IKE/IKZE is available to you).
5. **Path 3's return assumption is less robust.** Path 3's 9.96% blended
   return is a hand-averaged mix of two separate funds' trailing CAGRs
   (SPY and AGG), not one real instrument's own reported blended return like
   Paths 1–2's AOM figure. It also partly reflects an unusually strong
   recent equity bull-market window in the SPY figure. Treat Path 3's large
   margin as an optimistic upper bound, not the expected case, and note it
   carries more equity-concentration volatility than Paths 1–2.

## Recommendation

**Path 1 — Full Commitment, Moderate Balanced Allocation (AOM 40/60) — is
the recommended path.** It clears the conservative, horizon-appropriate
685,714.29 EUR bar by a substantial +49.7% margin, uses a real,
single, pre-blended diversified instrument (no synthetic blend or
assumed weighting), matches your stated moderate risk tolerance, and its
required contribution (5,787.25 EUR/month) is exactly your full confirmed
monthly surplus — it directly reflects your stated willingness to invest
the full amount, with no unnecessary optimism.

Path 2 is not a separate choice but a robustness check on Path 1: it
confirms the projection is not fragile to the exact CAGR chosen within
AOM's own reported range, and is worth keeping in mind as a "lower bound"
scenario. Path 3 is worth considering only if, having seen the caveat
above about its less-robust return assumption and extra volatility, you
are comfortable tilting toward the growth end of "moderate" — it is not the
primary recommendation. Path 4 should not be treated as a viable route to
the goal; it exists to make one thing clear: the comfortable margin in
Paths 1–3 depends entirely on actually investing the full stated
25,000 PLN/month, not a partial amount.

## Action Plan

- **Month 1:** Open (or confirm access to) a brokerage account capable of
  holding a moderate 40/60 balanced allocation fund (e.g. AOM or an
  equivalent low-cost 40/60 balanced ETF/fund available to you as a Polish
  resident). Confirm whether a tax-advantaged wrapper (e.g. IKE/IKZE) is
  available and worth using, given the unmodeled Belka tax caveat above.
- **Month 1–2:** Transfer and invest the full existing lump sum of
  200,000 PLN (46,298.00 EUR) from the PKO Bank Polski cash savings account
  into the chosen moderate 40/60 balanced allocation.
- **Month 1 onward:** Set up an automatic monthly investment of the full
  25,000 PLN/month (5,787.25 EUR/month) surplus into the same allocation.
  Treat this as non-negotiable — Path 4 shows that under-investing this
  amount by even 40% flips the goal from achievable to not achievable.
- **Every 12 months (Years 1–9):** Review that the full monthly contribution
  is still being made in full, and rebalance back to the target 40/60 (or
  chosen) allocation if it has drifted.
- **Year 5 (age 45) — Midpoint check-in:** Compare actual accumulated
  capital against the Path 1/Path 2 trajectory. If materially behind (e.g.
  tracking closer to Path 4's trajectory), revisit whether the full
  25,000 PLN/month is genuinely being invested and consider whether the
  target age or target income needs adjusting.
- **Year 8–9:** Begin researching the actual Belka (capital-gains) tax
  treatment and any available tax-advantaged account options for the
  withdrawal phase, and confirm your intended withdrawal-phase length so
  the 3.5% safe-withdrawal-rate assumption can be revisited against your
  actual retirement horizon rather than the ~35–45-year default assumed
  here.
- **Year 10 (age 50) — Goal date:** Reassess total accumulated capital
  against the then-current required capital (recompute the safe-withdrawal
  target using then-current safe withdrawal rate research and your
  confirmed withdrawal horizon) before beginning withdrawals.
