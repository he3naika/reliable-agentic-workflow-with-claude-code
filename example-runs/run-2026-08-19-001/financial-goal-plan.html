Financial Goal Plan — Target Passive Income of 2,000 EUR/month by Age 55

Executive Summary

**Verdict: the original goal — 2,000 EUR/month passive income by age 55 — is

not achievable with the current disposable surplus, but three concrete

adjustments make a version of it achievable.** Sustaining the full

483.74 EUR/month contribution (100% of current disposable surplus) at a

moderate 50/50 equity/bond index-fund allocation for the requested 20-year

horizon reaches only 239,295.64 EUR against the 600,000 EUR required at a 4%

safe withdrawal rate — a shortfall of 360,704.36 EUR. The recommended path

forward is **Path 3: a growth-tilted 70/30 equity/bond allocation, which

reaches the full 600,000 EUR target by age 66 (11 years later than

requested)** — conditional on the user explicitly confirming they accept a

more equity-heavy allocation than their stated "moderate" risk tolerance. If

that confirmation is not given, **Path 2 (strict 50/50 moderate allocation,

achievable by age 69)** is the recommended fallback that stays fully within

the stated risk tolerance. The single most important number: **600,000 EUR

of required capital**, against a currently projected 239,295.64 EUR at the

originally requested age and allocation.

Verdict

  • Path 1 — NOT achievable. Projected capital 239,295.64 EUR vs. 600,000

EUR required (≈39.9% of target) — shortfall of 360,704.36 EUR. Contribution

itself is affordable (equals full surplus), but return, horizon, and

contribution combined are mathematically insufficient by age 55.

  • Path 2 — Achievable. Projected capital 621,849.50 EUR ≥ 600,000 EUR

required at 34 years; 33 years is confirmed insufficient (584,176.85 EUR),

so 34 years is the minimum horizon at this rate/contribution. Achieved

strictly within the stated moderate risk tolerance, at the cost of

retiring 14 years later than requested.

  • **Path 3 — Achievable, but exceeds stated moderate risk tolerance as

currently scoped.** Projected capital 616,762.90 EUR ≥ 600,000 EUR

required at 31 years; 30 years is confirmed insufficient (574,392.46 EUR).

Mathematically sound with 11 fewer years of delay than Path 2, but the

70/30 allocation is more aggressive than the 50/50 split used as the

"moderate" baseline elsewhere and should not be adopted without the user's

explicit sign-off.

  • Path 4 — Achievable, but changes the goal itself. Projected capital

239,295.64 EUR clears the reduced 239,100 EUR requirement by a margin of

only 195.64 EUR. Matches the original target age and moderate risk

tolerance exactly, but only satisfies ≈40% of the originally requested

passive income (797 EUR/month vs. 2,000 EUR/month).

  • Cross-cutting risk (all paths): the required monthly contribution

equals 100% of the current disposable surplus, leaving zero margin for an

expense shock, a bad month, or the unconfirmed tax-regime/ZUS-relief

assumptions in net income turning out less favorable. All EUR figures also

carry unmodeled FX risk, since the PLN/EUR rate used is a single

point-in-time spot quote and Poland is not in the Eurozone.

Recommendation

**Primary recommendation: Path 3 (growth-tilted 70/30 allocation, achievable

by age 66)** — of the paths that preserve the full 2,000 EUR/month target,

this is the smallest deviation from the requested target age (11 years later

vs. 14 for Path 2), and every projection has been independently recomputed

and confirmed. This is recommended **conditional on the user explicitly

confirming** they accept a 70/30 equity/bond split, since it is more

equity-heavy than the 50/50 allocation their stated "moderate" risk

tolerance would otherwise imply.

**Fallback recommendation: Path 2 (strict 50/50 moderate allocation,

achievable by age 69)** — if the user declines the higher equity weighting

in Path 3, this path reaches the full 600,000 EUR target while staying

strictly within the stated moderate risk tolerance, at the cost of the

largest delay (14 years) among the achievable paths.

Path 4 (reduced target, original age) remains available as a distinct

option if the target retirement age of 55 is the least negotiable

constraint — but note it satisfies a materially smaller goal (≈797

EUR/month, not 2,000 EUR/month). Path 1 is not recommendable as-is; it is

retained only to show why a lever (horizon, allocation, or target) must

change.

All paths assume the full 483.74 EUR/month contribution is sustained without

interruption for the entire projection horizon, and that the PLN/EUR rate

and the net-income assumptions (tax regime, ZUS relief eligibility) do not

materially change from what is documented in Current Position above.

Action Plan

1. Month 1: Confirm with an accountant or tax advisor which PIT regime

is actually elected (ryczałt ewidencjonowany 12% vs. podatek liniowy 19%

vs. skala podatkowa) and whether any ZUS start-up relief window applies —

this validates or corrects the 8,083.71 PLN/month net income figure this

plan is built on.

2. Month 1: Decide between the two primary paths — confirm explicitly

whether a 70/30 growth-tilted allocation (Path 3, target age 66) is

acceptable, or whether to stay with the strict 50/50 moderate allocation

(Path 2, target age 69).

3. Month 1–2: Open a brokerage account capable of holding UCITS ETFs

(for EU/Polish tax residents) and select the concrete instruments: an

equity sleeve tracking the MSCI World Index (e.g. iShares Core MSCI

World UCITS ETF, ticker SWDA/URTH) and a bond sleeve tracking an

aggregate bond index (e.g. iShares Core U.S. Aggregate Bond ETF, AGG, or

a EUR-hedged aggregate-bond UCITS equivalent), weighted per the path

chosen in step 2 (50/50 or 70/30).

4. Month 2: Move the existing 50,000 PLN savings-account balance

(≈11,607.65 EUR) into the chosen allocation as the starting principal,

keeping a separate cash buffer for short-term expenses outside this

investment plan.

5. Month 2 onward: Set up an automatic monthly transfer of the full

disposable surplus (2,083.71 PLN ≈ 483.74 EUR) into the chosen

equity/bond allocation, split according to the confirmed path's weights.

6. Ongoing, annually: Re-check actual net income against the 8,083.71

PLN/month assumption (especially after the tax-regime confirmation in

step 1), and re-check the disposable surplus against the 6,000 PLN/month

expense assumption — because the required contribution equals 100% of

the current surplus, any negative variance should be caught early rather

than silently eroding the plan's margin.

7. Ongoing, annually: Re-check the PLN/EUR exchange rate against the

NBP-sourced 4.3075 baseline used here, since it is a single spot quote

and Poland is not in the Eurozone; material currency moves over the

20–34 year horizon should trigger a plan recalculation.

8. Every 3–5 years: Recompute progress-to-target using a future-value

projection with actual contributions and actual market returns to date,

to confirm the chosen path (age 66 or 69) is still on track, rather than

assuming the original projection holds unchanged for the full

multi-decade horizon.

9. At the target age (66 or 69, per the confirmed path): Reassess the

accumulated capital against the 600,000 EUR requirement before initiating

withdrawals at the 4% safe withdrawal rate, since the SWR itself carries

a documented range (3.5%–4.7% across cited sources) that may warrant a

final review at that time.
